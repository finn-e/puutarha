# Sleep consolidation is critical for memory formation

During REM and slow-wave sleep, the brain actively restructures and stores the day’s learning into long-term memory. Rest is a biological imperative, supported by how [[Human-animal bonds reduce physiological stress markers]] to aid recovery.[^1][^1]

[^1]: Walker, M. P., & Stickgold, R. (2006). Sleep, memory, and plasticity. Annual Review of Psychology.
